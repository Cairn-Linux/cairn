Brand guidelines · v0.1 draft September 2026

cairn

A computer that grows with your child.

A cairn is a small stack of stones left by someone who walked the trail first — a marker that helps whoever comes next without walking the path for them. That is the brand.

01

The idea

Tools, not toys. The brand reads as a creative workspace a child is trusted with — not a toy that talks down to them. Respect reads as capability faster than cartoon mascots do.

Calm and deterministic. Flat shapes, a restrained fjord palette on a warm sand ground, generous space. Nothing glossy, nothing shouting, nothing animated for its own sake.

Same language at every age. One visual identity carries a child from 5 to 14. Nothing here is "for little kids" that a twelve-year-old would be embarrassed by.

No mascots No gradients No rainbow No gamification No baby talk
02

The mark

Four stacked stones, drawn as rounded bars. Each stone rests on a wider one — stable, legible at 16px, and buildable by a child out of paper strips. C — Trail stack — is the mark: offset like a real cairn, hand-placed, clearly not a corporate glyph. A and B remain below as the explored record; the Tweaks panel previews each on the cover.

Clear space

Keep one base-stone height of empty space on all sides. Nothing enters it — not even the tagline.

Minimum size

Mark alone: 16px tall (favicon). With wordmark: 24px tall. Below that, use the mark only.

One ink

The mark is Ink on light grounds, Sand on dark grounds. Never outlined, rotated, gradient-filled, or given a face.

03

Color

A fjord palette: deep teal ink, one blue accent, warm sand ground. Five colors total — restrained by design (§6.2). Two of them exist only to color-code what a tile does.

Sand #F4EDE0 is the everyday ground; Paper #FBF7EF for cards on it. Ink is the only dark ground (terminal, login).

Rules

Color codes kind, never app: ochre = make, moss = practice, fjord = machine. Body text is always Ink on Sand (12.9:1). Ochre and Moss are fills for large shapes only, never text. No gradients, ever.

04

Typography

One family everywhere: Atkinson Hyperlegible, designed by the Braille Institute to disambiguate letterforms — which matters disproportionately for early readers. Its Mono sibling covers the terminal. No display face; the wordmark is just the Bold.

Heading · Bold 28

Body · Regular 17 / 1.65

> open tuxpaint
Why this face
Il1 O0 B8

Every ambiguous pair is drawn distinct: capital I has serifs, l has a tail, 0 is slashed. A six-year-old sounding out a command can't mistake one for another.

Sizing rules

Child-facing UI: 18px minimum, 1.5 line height, sentence case. Guardian/docs: 16px minimum. Never all-caps for content — kickers and labels only. Weights: 400 and 700 only.

05

Voice

The system talks like a calm adult who assumes the child is capable. It suggests, never scolds; it names real things with real words. Simplicity comes from short sentences and a small vocabulary — never from baby talk. Two audiences, one tone; the parent copy is just longer.

Say this
> opn tuxpaint
I don't know "opn" — did you mean open?

"Something needs a grown-up." · "That's saved in your folder." · "All done — want to keep going?"

Never this
> opn tuxpaint
bash: opn: command not found (exit 127)

No stack traces, no errnos, no "Oops!!" with three exclamation marks, no baby talk, no fake enthusiasm, no exclamation-mark rewards.

Real words

"open", "files", "save" — never invented kid-verbs. The vocabulary must transfer to a real system later.

Calm failure

Every error path leads somewhere boring and safe. Errors suggest the next step; they never assign blame.

Straight with parents

State limitations plainly (no remote management, hardware floor). A parent should never discover a caveat.

06

In use

The first surfaces the brand must carry: the login screen and the L1 launcher. These are sketches of the brand applied, not UI specs.
